# Crime-data
**Project: Chicago Crime Data Analysis (2022)**

Objective:
Analyzed 2022 crime data to evaluate the impact of a government-led operation aimed at reducing crime. Assessed case-solving efficiency, identified high-crime neighborhoods, and provided data-driven recommendations for improved policing and community safety.

Process:
Cleaned data by removing duplicates and blank entries.
Used DAX functions for calculations and Power BI for visualization.
Created interactive dashboards (Main, Domestic, Location) with filters, charts, and slicers for dynamic analysis.

Key Insights:
Top Crimes: Theft (22.4%), Battery (18.9%), and Criminal Damage (11%) constituted 52% of total crimes.
Seasonal Trends: Crime peaked in April (15.6K cases) but dropped 67% after a police operation in May.
Hotspots: 30% of crimes occurred near apartments/residences; alleys and parking lots were vulnerable.
Domestic Crimes: Made up 20% of cases but had only 13% arrest rates, indicating enforcement gaps.
Efficiency: Average case resolution time was 6.98 days (below the national 10.2-day average).

Recommendations:
Targeted Policing: Increase patrols in high-crime areas (streets, residences, parking lots).
Domestic Violence Response: Strengthen interventions in District 6 and improve arrest rates.
Resource Allocation: Focus on districts with low arrest rates (e.g., District 8) for better enforcement.

Impact:
Provided actionable insights to optimize law enforcement strategies, reduce crime rates, and enhance public safety.
